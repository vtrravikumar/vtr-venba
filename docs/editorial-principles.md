# Editorial Principles

## 1. Originality and relationship to tradition

This is an original work in English that takes formal inspiration from the discipline of Tamil **குறள் வெண்பா (Kural Venba)**. It is not a translation of the Thirukkural and must not be marketed or described as one. It should not simply reproduce Thiruvalluvar's ideas in new wording.

The project should honour the tradition through careful study of its form, while allowing its subjects and insights to address human experience from 1900–2027.

## 2. Human experience without a single prescribed life

Readers should be able to recognise their own experience in the work regardless of whether they marry, have children, pursue a conventional career, follow a religion, or choose a particular way of living. Chapter framing and verse topics must not treat one life path as mandatory or universal.

The age ranges assigned to Parts are organising lenses, not rigid claims about when a person must experience a particular event or attain a particular kind of wisdom.

## 3. Distinct Chapter territories

Before any kurals are written:

1. Write a short scope statement for every Chapter.
2. Identify what belongs in that Chapter and what belongs elsewhere.
3. Maintain a topic register for all planned verses.
4. Track repeated images, claims, moral lessons, and conclusions.
5. Review overlaps across Chapters, not just within each Part.

A recurring subject is acceptable only when the Chapter's question, context, or perspective is clearly distinct. For example, ambition may be considered as youthful drive in one place and as a later reassessment of priorities elsewhere, but the verses must not merely repeat the same advice.

## 4. Kural Venba grammar and English

Traditional Kural Venba is governed by Tamil prosodic rules. English syllables or word counts cannot simply be substituted for Tamil metrical units and assumed to produce a valid form.

Before verse composition, the project must research and document a defensible method for handling English pronunciation in relation to Tamil prosody. The method should clearly distinguish:

- Tamil metrical concepts and rules as traditionally defined.
- The pronunciation assumptions used when evaluating English words.
- Any adaptations proposed for an English-language work.
- What can be validated mechanically and what requires informed human review.

The method must be transparent about limitations. Do not label a verse as formally compliant merely because it has two lines or appears compact.

## 5. Validation checklist

Each proposed kural should be reviewed for the applicable traditional requirements, including:

- The two-line structure and the expected four seer in the first line and three in the second.
- The composition of asai (நேர் and நிரை) and seer.
- Thalai (தளை) relationships between seer.
- The permitted ending / ஈற்றுச்சீர் requirements.
- Any other applicable rules established by authoritative prosodic references.

This list is a reminder of areas to validate, not a substitute for a complete grammar specification. The exact rules and evaluation procedure must be researched before they are treated as settled.

Record each verse's status honestly, such as **draft**, **prosody review needed**, **revised**, or **validated**. A tool may assist with consistency checks, but automated checks alone should not be treated as proof of metrical correctness.

## 6. Quality of thought and language

- Prefer precise, memorable insight over vague aphorism.
- Avoid preaching, easy certainty, and simplistic universal claims.
- Allow contradiction, ambiguity, tenderness, humour, and uncertainty where human experience calls for them.
- Keep each verse intelligible in English while respecting the chosen formal method.
- Avoid filler written only to reach the target of ten kurals per Chapter.
- Do not sacrifice truth or clarity to make a line appear metrically convenient.

## 7. Working sequence

1. Confirm the overall architecture.
2. Define the scope and boundaries of all 72 Chapters.
3. Build and maintain the topic register.
4. Research the form and document the English-language validation method.
5. Establish the manuscript front matter and publishing conventions.
6. Draft kurals Chapter by Chapter.
7. Review literary quality and thematic distinctness.
8. Validate prosody, revise, and record the status of every verse.
9. Prepare the manuscript for the VTR Press workflow.

## 8. Editorial record

Keep documentation practical and proportionate. Add a new document only when it supports a concrete editorial, research, validation, or production need.
