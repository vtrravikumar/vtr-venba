# Editorial Principles

## 1. Equal literary standing and originality

This is an original bilingual literary work in Tamil and English. The two languages have equal literary standing; neither is defined as merely the translation of the other.

Each paired composition begins with a shared central insight. The Tamil and English versions should express that insight in ways that are natural, memorable, and artistically effective in their own languages. They need not share identical syntax, imagery, or word order, but neither should contradict, flatten, or materially change the core thought.

The work takes formal inspiration from traditional Tamil **குறள் வெண்பா (Kural Venba)**. It is not a translation of the Thirukkural and must not be marketed or described as one. It should not simply reproduce Thiruvalluvar's ideas in new wording. The subjects and insights should address human experience across the modern period from 1900–2027.

## 2. Human experience without a single prescribed life

Readers should be able to recognise their own experience in the work regardless of whether they marry, have children, pursue a conventional career, follow a religion, or choose a particular way of living. Chapter framing and verse topics must not treat one life path as mandatory or universal.

The age ranges assigned to Parts are organising lenses, not rigid claims about when a person must experience a particular event or attain a particular kind of wisdom.

## 3. Distinct Chapter territories

Before any verses are written:

1. Write a short scope statement for every Chapter.
2. Identify what belongs in that Chapter and what belongs elsewhere.
3. Maintain a topic register for all planned paired compositions.
4. Track repeated images, claims, moral lessons, and conclusions across both languages.
5. Review overlaps across Chapters, not just within each Part.
6. Record the shared insight for each pair, so both language versions remain aligned without becoming literal translations.

A recurring subject is acceptable only when the Chapter's question, context, or perspective is clearly distinct. For example, ambition may be considered as youthful drive in one place and as a later reassessment of priorities elsewhere, but the verses must not merely repeat the same advice.

## 4. Traditional Tamil prosody and English composition

Traditional Kural Venba is governed by Tamil prosodic rules. The Tamil compositions must be evaluated against those rules, using authoritative references and knowledgeable review.

English pronunciation, syllables, stress, or word counts cannot simply be substituted for Tamil metrical units and assumed to produce a valid Kural Venba. The English compositions therefore need a separately documented compositional and prosodic method. The project must be transparent about whether English verses follow a defined adaptation, echo selected structural features, or use another clearly described approach.

Before verse composition, document:

- The traditional Tamil metrical concepts and rules as established by reliable sources.
- The pronunciation assumptions used when evaluating Tamil words and verses.
- The intended form and evaluation criteria for the English companion verses.
- Which checks can be automated and which require informed human review.
- The precise limits of any claim that the English verses reproduce or adapt the Tamil form.

Do not claim that the English verses satisfy traditional Tamil prosody unless a defensible method establishes what that claim means and the verses have been reviewed accordingly.

## 5. Tamil validation checklist

Each Tamil composition should be reviewed for the applicable traditional requirements, including:

- The two-line structure and the expected four seer in the first line and three in the second.
- The composition of asai (நேர் and நிரை) and seer.
- Thalai (தளை) relationships between seer.
- The permitted ending / ஈற்றுச்சீர் requirements.
- Any other applicable rules established by authoritative prosodic references.

This list is a reminder of areas to validate, not a substitute for a complete grammar specification. The exact rules and evaluation procedure must be researched before they are treated as settled.

## 6. Pair-level and language-level quality

Review every pair at three levels:

1. **Shared insight:** Do both verses convey the intended central thought?
2. **Tamil composition:** Is the Tamil natural, compelling, and metrically valid according to the documented traditional rules?
3. **English composition:** Is the English natural and memorable, and does it meet the project's separately documented formal aims?

Neither version should be forced into awkward language just to resemble the other. Differences in imagery or phrasing are acceptable when they serve the same insight. Meaningful disagreement between the versions is not.

Record each verse's status honestly, such as **concept**, **draft**, **language review needed**, **prosody review needed**, **revised**, or **validated**. A tool may assist with consistency checks, but automated checks alone should not be treated as proof of metrical correctness.

## 7. Quality of thought and language

- Prefer precise, memorable insight over vague aphorism.
- Avoid preaching, easy certainty, and simplistic universal claims.
- Allow contradiction, ambiguity, tenderness, humour, and uncertainty where human experience calls for them.
- Use idiomatic, literary language in both Tamil and English.
- Avoid filler written only to reach the target of ten pairs per Chapter.
- Do not sacrifice truth or clarity to make a line appear metrically convenient.
- Treat both languages as creative works in their own right, while preserving the integrity of the shared insight.

## 8. Working sequence

1. Confirm the overall architecture.
2. Define the scope and boundaries of all 72 Chapters.
3. Build and maintain the shared-insight and topic register.
4. Research traditional Tamil Kural Venba and document the English compositional method.
5. Establish the manuscript front matter and bilingual presentation conventions.
6. Draft paired compositions Chapter by Chapter.
7. Review literary quality, thematic distinctness, and alignment of each pair.
8. Validate Tamil prosody, review the English form against its stated method, revise, and record each verse's status.
9. Prepare the manuscript for the VTR Press workflow.

## 9. Editorial record

Keep documentation practical and proportionate. Add a new document only when it supports a concrete editorial, research, validation, or production need.
